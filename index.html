Methodology

The Process

A systematic approach to inventory health scoring, built entirely in Google Sheets using custom formulas and weighted scoring algorithms.

Design Philosophy: Asymmetric Penalty System

The core insight behind this scoring system is that understocking is more damaging than overstocking. When a customer can't find their size, they leave empty-handed—and may not return. Overstock creates markdown risk, but at least the sale opportunity exists.

This system penalizes inventory gaps (understock) more heavily than excess inventory, ensuring scores reflect real business risk. A category with 20% understock will score significantly worse than one with 20% overstock.

Data Aggregation & Normalization

Raw sales and inventory data is aggregated from multiple sources, then normalized to comparable percentages for cross-category analysis.

// Sales share calculation
= SUMIF(Brand_Column, Brand_Name, Sales_Column) / SUM(Sales_Column)
// Inventory share calculation
= COUNTIF(Size_Column, Target_Size) / COUNTA(Size_Column)

Weighted Scoring Algorithm

Each category receives a weighted score based on inventory vs sales alignment. The formula applies asymmetric penalties favoring overstock scenarios.

// Alignment score with asymmetric penalty
= IF(Inventory_Share < Sales_Share,
// Understock penalty (steeper curve)
100 - ABS(Deviation) * 1.5,
// Overstock penalty (gentler curve)
100 - ABS(Deviation) * 0.8)

Strategic Analysis

Key Findings

Critical insights uncovered through systematic data analysis and cross-referencing.

Core Size Crisis

Core sizes represent 72% of population demand but receive only 39-45% of inventory allocation. This systematic understocking of the most common sizes is the primary driver of lost sales.

Top Sellers Perform Better

Counterintuitively, the Top 10 sellers show better size distribution than overall inventory. High demand pressure triggers more frequent reorders, naturally improving distribution balance.

Men's Outperforms Women's

Men's inventory scores significantly higher (85 B) compared to Women's (68 D). This suggests better demand forecasting or more stable purchasing patterns in Men's categories.

Neutral Trainer Dominance

The Neutral Trainer category represents 57-60% of all sales and shows the best alignment scores. This category should remain the primary focus for inventory investment and optimization.

Individual Category Gaps

All individual categories score in the F range (32-58), even though the weighted totals show moderate performance. This reveals systemic issues across all categories that are being masked by aggregation.

Category Range 32-58

Actionable Priorities

Immediate focus: Replenish core sizes (Women's 7.5-9.5, Men's 9.5-11.5) for top sellers. Secondary: Address width availability gaps, particularly Wide (D) sizes which show 25% inventory vs 62% demand.
